## Result and scope

A one-link budget was applied within each of four curated Docker development
groups. All thirteen candidates passed blinded Haiku and Sol reviews of complete
source and destination pages; only one group had competing highest-tier options.
All eleven alternatives there scored 6/6 on Haiku's relationship-support,
target-support and placement rubric. The semantic comparator used this same
supported tier and a fixed deterministic tie order.

At $q=.25$, finite unit-phase marginal energy on changed biconnected blocks,
divided by the original group's fixed edge-weight sum, separated eight continuous
integration and delivery (CI/CD) additions (raw cost $.194407$) from three testing
additions ($.401924$). Restricting the tie order to the eight tied minima selected
C++ development $\to$ CI/CD instead of .NET development $\to$ unit tests.
Cost did not distinguish C++ from the other seven minima. Actual edits and matched
numerical replay were verified.

The selector was developed after inspecting these examples; blinded AI reviews
followed that exposed development. This demonstrates changed selection in one
of four groups, without human validation or independent replication. The cost
uses the magnetic operator's weights and phases; it is neither a measured change
in normalized $\lambda_1$ nor a certified global optimum. Retrieval and answer
benefit were not tested.

## Method

The thirteen inputs are purposively curated additions in four fixed development
source-family groups (830, 53, 6 and 8 pages; 897 total), from the substantive
link-mask view. These differ from the historical whole-corpus LT graph view.
Each addition wraps an existing prose phrase without changing its words, code,
lists, navigation or existing links; neither orientation already exists, and
endpoints share a baseline weak component of the induced group. No reserved
group is used. The comparison unit is a group, not an independent link trial.

Claude Haiku 5.5 and gpt-6.1-sol receive complete source/target pages in isolated
requests without curator labels, magnetic values or prior judgments. Haiku sets
the semantic tier; Sol confirms eligibility. Each reviewer scores relationship
support, target support and placement from 0 to 2. Admission requires every
dimension at least 1, total at least 5, and no uncertainty or contradiction.
All thirteen pass both gates. Extensions has Haiku score 5/6; the other twelve
have 6/6. Agreement is not human accuracy; repeated guide templates and these
development groups are not independent replications. Historical curator labels
and the earlier v2 selection result are distinct from this fresh common pool.

With the paper's binary directed adjacency $A$, use
$W=(A+A^{\mathsf T})/2$ and $\theta_{ij}=2\pi q(A_{ij}-A_{ji})$.
The unit-phase energy and desired marginal quantity are
\[
E_G(\phi)=\sum_{i<j}2W_{ij}[1-\cos(\phi_i-\phi_j-\theta_{ij})],
\]
\[
F(G)=\min_\phi E_G(\phi),\qquad
\Delta(P)=\frac{F(G+P)-F(G)}{B},\qquad B=\sum_{\text{old }i<j}W_{ij}.
\]
The original group's $B$ stays fixed across candidates. Unchanged biconnected
edge blocks cancel; changed blocks are solved jointly, with bridges counted
as blocks. Free gauge rotations at articulation vertices justify block
additivity for this phase-difference energy. Multiple edits cannot generally
be scored by summing their isolated marginal costs. Here the budget is one.
The language group's $B=21$; raw costs $.194407$ and $.401924$ correspond to
normalized costs $.00925746$ and $.01913923$. Compare raw costs within a group,
not across groups with different denominators.

For unit phases $z_i=e^{i\phi_i}$ and $y=D^{1/2}z$, the normalized Rayleigh
quotient is $E_G(\phi)/(2B)$. The normalized eigenproblem allows varying
amplitudes. Degrees change on addition; this selector does not measure a
decrease in normalized $\lambda_1$. At a true optimum the nonnegative added
terms cannot lower total optimal energy. A smaller marginal cost does not
establish repair of an existing inconsistency.

Two fixed schedules (seeds 3501 and 3502), each with eight deterministic starts,
provide finite estimates. L-BFGS-B uses maxiter 2000, ftol $10^{-15}$, gtol
$10^{-8}$ and maxls 40; full-gradient infinity norm must be at most $10^{-7}$.
The lowest-energy stationary result is retained within each schedule. Energy
identity tolerance is $10^{-9}$. Stationarity does not certify a minimum.
Schedule extrema are sensitivity ranges, not confidence intervals or error
bounds. The difference of feasible energy estimates is not a bound on the
true marginal optimum. The numerical policy is v2; selection uses the separately
versioned \texttt{embedded\_selection.minimum\_set.v1} rule.

With raw $\epsilon=10^{-6}$, a tied minimum set must have envelope spread at
most $\epsilon$, and every outsider must lie strictly beyond its upper envelope
plus $\epsilon$. The fixed semantic order chooses within that set. An unavailable
highest-tier alternative or unresolved overlap forces whole-tier semantic
fallback; no difficult alternative is dropped. Resource exhaustion takes
precedence. S is the semantic comparator; analytic $q=0$ control Z equals S and
is not an optimized ordinary-navigation or retrieval-utility competitor.
The frozen order is descending Haiku score, then tie ID, then proposal ID.
The eleven language options' order is 41-06, 41-03, 41-12, 41-01, 41-04,
41-11, 41-09, 41-13, 41-07, 41-02, 41-08. Restricting it to the eight minima
chooses 41-12. Their recorded envelope spread is
$3.6637359812630166\times10^{-14}$ and outsider gap $.20751705233494078$.
The earlier v2 rule fell back over the full tier for this partial tie and
changed no selection. The minimum-set rule was developed after seeing that
outcome; subsequent blinded reviews do not make it an unseen replication.

All four original/S/Z/M copies contain 897 files. The recorded independent
verification checks exact patches, graph changes, visible text, assignments and
externally anchored ledgers; the changed eleven-candidate tier was replayed
with unchanged numerical settings. Selected cards were inspected. This note
transcribes those records; it does not rerun the scorers or model reviews.
Source previews are not full rendered-site verification.

## Structural explanation

The eight lower-cost language additions have exact directed before/after block
mappings to C++ 41-12; the three higher-cost additions map to .NET 41-06.
The higher-cost edit joins two bridges into a transitive triangle. The lower-cost
edit joins a bridge to an existing transitive triangle, making two triangles
share an edge. Cycles here are in the underlying undirected support; neither
edit creates a directed cycle. The scorer does not read CI/CD/testing labels;
their alignment here is a property of these curated structures.

For .NET 41-06 the original arcs are develop $\to$ containerize and run-tests
$\to$ containerize; the addition is develop $\to$ run-tests. For C++ 41-12 the
original arcs are develop $\to$ containerize, deploy $\to$ configure-ci-cd,
deploy $\to$ containerize and configure-ci-cd $\to$ containerize; the addition
is develop $\to$ configure-ci-cd.

The recorded triangle expression is $3(1-\cos(\pi/6))=.4019237886466839$.
For the diamond, minimize $5-\sin x-4\cos(x/2)$ at
$\sin(x/2)=(\sqrt3-1)/2$. Its recorded edited energy
$.5963305249583879$ minus the old triangle energy gives $.19440673631170402$.
The saved analytic comparisons explain the repeated small structures without
certifying a general global solver.
